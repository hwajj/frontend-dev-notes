전체적으로 자연스럽고 회화체도 꽤 잘 잡혀 있어. 다만 몇 군데는 **문법적으로 틀렸다기보다 원어민이 실제로 말할 때 더 자연스러운 표현**으로 바꾸는 게 좋아. 특히 `told me that something ... was actually assigned` 부분과 `she thinks Emma is a great coworker`, `passing along that` 같은 부분을 다듬었어.

그리고 제목도 **Stuck in the Middle**은 아주 자연스러워서 그대로 두면 돼.

### 4/23 목

**Title:** Stuck in the Middle
**제목:** 중간에서 난처한 상황

You remember my coworker, right? The one who’s into fitness and always talks about marathons. She’s actually a really decent person—kind, capable, and a good team player. Her name is Emma.

And there’s someone from another team who helped me adjust when I first joined the company. She’s about ten years older than me, and her tone can be a bit blunt, but she’s warm at heart. Her name is Sophia.

Anyway, since our team often works with her team, we sometimes clash. Sophia can be pretty blunt, and she takes a lot of pride in her work. She has over ten years of experience, so she sometimes looks down on what we do—like, “I’ve done all that before.” Because of that, Emma has gradually started to dislike her. And Sophia has no idea.

Today, I had lunch with Sophia for the first time in a while. She told me that something she had agreed to handle in yesterday’s meeting had actually been assigned to our team. I told her that wasn’t the case, but she insisted she was right and asked me to tell Emma that it was our team’s responsibility. After lunch, I went back to my desk, and apparently Sophia ran into Emma and told her directly, “That’s your team’s task.”

Emma checked with management to confirm what had been decided in the meeting, and they also said it was Sophia’s responsibility. So now Emma is upset because Sophia kept insisting that she was right.

I kind of wish Sophia would apologize to Emma. I like Sophia, and I don’t want her to be disliked. And actually, Sophia once told me that she thought Emma was a great coworker. She can come off as strong and sometimes seems a bit intimidating, but I know she’s genuinely a warm person.

Now I’m stuck in the middle. If I tell her to apologize, it might sound like I’m stirring things up or telling her that Emma is upset with her. But right now, Sophia doesn’t even realize she’s mistaken. If I don’t say anything and she keeps insisting she’s right in tomorrow’s meeting, that could make things worse. But if she later finds out that I knew she was mistaken and didn’t tell her, she might feel hurt.

### 자주 쓰는 표현 정리 (영한)

| 표현                    | 뜻(회화 뉘앙스)                             | 예문                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **stuck in the middle** | 중간에서 난처하다, 양쪽 사이에 끼다         | “I’m **stuck in the middle** here.”                         |
| **a decent person**     | 성품이 괜찮은 사람, 꽤 괜찮은 사람          | “She’s actually a really **decent person**.”                |
| **a good team player**  | 팀워크가 좋은 사람, 같이 일하기 좋은 동료   | “She’s capable and **a good team player**.”                 |
| **help someone adjust** | 누군가가 적응하도록 돕다                    | “She **helped me adjust** when I first joined the company.” |
| **blunt**               | (말투가) 직설적이다, 다소 거칠다            | “She can be pretty **blunt**.”                              |
| **clash**               | 부딪히다, 충돌하다                          | “We sometimes **clash** at work.”                           |
| **take pride in ~**     | ~에 자부심을 갖다                           | “She **takes a lot of pride in her work**.”                 |
| **look down on ~**      | ~을 얕보다, 깔보다                          | “She sometimes **looks down on** what we do.”               |
| **insist (that) ~**     | ~라고 우기다, 강하게 주장하다               | “She **insisted she was right**.”                           |
| **run into ~**          | ~을 우연히 마주치다                         | “She **ran into Emma** after lunch.”                        |
| **check with ~**        | ~에게 확인하다                              | “Emma **checked with management**.”                         |
| **come off as ~**       | ~한 인상을 주다                             | “She can **come off as strong**.”                           |
| **intimidating**        | 위압적인, 무섭게 느껴지는                   | “She can seem a little **intimidating**.”                   |
| **genuinely warm**      | 진심으로 따뜻한                             | “She’s **genuinely warm** at heart.”                        |
| **stir things up**      | 괜히 일을 키우다, 상황을 더 복잡하게 만들다 | “I don’t want to **stir things up**.”                       |
| **pass along ~**        | 말·정보를 전달하다                          | “I don’t want to **pass along** what Emma said.”            |

몇 가지 핵심 수정 이유만 짚으면:

- **something she had agreed to handle ... was actually assigned → had actually been assigned**
  여기서는 `something`이 **배정된 대상**이라 수동태가 자연스러워.
- **she told me that she thinks Emma is... → she once told me that she thought Emma was...**
  과거에 했던 말을 전달하는 상황이라 시제를 맞추면 더 자연스러워.
- **come off strong → come off as strong**
  `come off as + 형용사/명사` 형태가 기본이라 `as`를 넣는 게 좋아.
- **passing along that Emma is upset → telling her that Emma is upset with her**
  `pass along` 자체는 맞지만 이 상황에서는 약간 정보 전달문 같은 느낌이라 `tell her`가 훨씬 일상적인 회화야.
- **they also believed it was Sophia’s responsibility → they also said it was Sophia’s responsibility**
  여기서는 관리자가 실제로 확인해준 상황이라 `said`가 더 직접적이고 자연스러워.

그리고 원문에서 **“I kind of wish Sophia would apologize to Emma”**는 아주 좋은 표현이야. `I want Sophia to apologize`보다 훨씬 부드럽고, “사과했으면 좋겠는데…” 하는 화자의 난처한 감정이 잘 살아 있어.
